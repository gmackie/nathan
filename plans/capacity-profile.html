Five thousand protected requests/second is a starting hypothesis for roughly one request/second from about 10% of the registered population. One thousand sign-in flows represents a login event involving about 2% of users at once. Five hundred apps exercises administration beyond the current 100-client and 200-app list caps. These assumptions need pilot data and load results.

Workload definitions and supporting dataset

Sign-in concurrency is a count of unfinished journeys, including simulated user wait. It is not completed logins/second or authorization-endpoint requests/second. Measure each separately. Propose independent arrival-rate scenarios of 50 starts/second for 30 minutes and 100 starts/second for 5 minutes; use synthetic test identities and controlled providers. Simulated wait and MFA paths are explicit workload inputs. A growing queue or insufficient load-generator capacity cannot count as a successful run.

Protected requests are aggregate traffic through Nathan-controlled gateway or binding authorization paths. Run each path separately, then a representative mix. Use warm/cold caches and a skewed distribution with 20% of requests on one hot app; report per-app saturation as well as the aggregate. Separate upstream business-app work from Nathan's added latency.

Proposed supporting dataset: 1,000 flat groups, 510,000 memberships (10 per user, including a broad group containing all 51,000 users) and 10,200 active app sessions. Measure an active-session-size sweep separately; larger persistent session populations must not be inferred from this baseline. Assign app grants through users and groups and cover both access modes and MFA settings. These values remain provisional.

Use actual Worker, Grafana and independent reference-app fixtures for compatibility. The remaining app registrations and test users are synthetic. Five hundred registrations does not imply five hundred deployed gateways. Keep a small real Google/GitHub/Entra acceptance suite separate from controlled load; never count a mock-provider throughput run as live federation compatibility.

Proposed test sequence

1 · ISOLATE BOTTLENECKS

Test directory, authentication and protected traffic independently

Validate complete search/pagination and grant selection at 51,000 users / 500 apps. Run the login concurrency and arrival-rate scenarios separately, plus token refresh at 100 exchanges/second for 30 minutes and 200/second for 5 minutes. Keep rate limiting enabled and exercise users sharing an office network as a separate case.

2 · COMBINE LOAD AND POLICY CHANGES

Hold baseline for 30 minutes, then burst for 5 minutes

Ramp over 5 minutes to 5,000 protected requests/second while holding 1,000 sign-in flows in progress. Run a separate 5-minute burst at 10,000 protected requests/second and 2,000 flows. During both phases, propose 10 membership/grant changes per second and test user blocks, app disable and broad-group grant removal.

Measure from authoritative mutation commit to denial. Continued traffic, old in-flight responses, cold isolates, D1 read consistency and revalidation errors must not extend an allow beyond 60 seconds.

3 · SOAK, RECOVER AND FIND LIMITS

Run four hours at 1,000 protected requests/second

Track sessions, database growth, cleanup, key-cache behavior and operational cost. Exercise revocation and recovery during the soak. Finish with a stepwise stress ramp to record the first limit and recovery behavior; the stress result is separate from passing the proposed baseline and burst.

Confirm the generator is not the bottleneck. Record actual Workers/D1 quotas, account plan, regions, bindings and versions. Any concurrency, locking or throughput limitation must lead to a revised architecture or explicitly revised target, with evidence.
